You can climb in Tenerife all year, which is exactly why climbers from Austria, Germany, the UK and Scandinavia come here in winter. The island sits at about 28° N, off the coast of Africa, so the south gets sun on most days and the average temperature only varies by about 6–7 °C across the year. The key is matching the crag to the season.

## The short answer

- **October to May**: the main season. Ideal on the south-coast crags (Arico, El Río, Tamadaya, Fasnia).
- **November to March**: peak season for European winter-sun climbers. Book guided days and Guaria permits early.
- **June to September**: hot on the coast. Climb at sunrise, follow the shade, or go high to Teide National Park.

## Sun, shade and wind

The southern gorges (*barrancos*) have walls facing many directions, so on any day there's usually sun **and** shade somewhere. In winter, pick sunny walls for the morning. In warm months, chase the shade. The trade winds (*alisios*) blow from the north-east and can make exposed crags cold in winter or welcome in summer, so a windproof layer always goes in the pack.

## Calima (Saharan dust)

A few times a year an easterly wind brings hot, dusty air from the Sahara (*calima*). Visibility drops, temperatures rise and the air feels heavy. On calima days, climb early, keep it short, and drink more. If it's extreme, take a rest day.

## North vs south

The north of the island is greener and is often under a "sea of clouds" in winter, so it's more humid and cloudy. Most climbing and the most reliable weather are in the **south and south-east**.

## Altitude: Teide National Park

Cañada del Capricho sits at around 2,000 m inside Teide National Park. It's the best place to climb in summer, but in winter it can be freezing, with ice or snow. The sun at altitude is very strong in every season. Remember the [reservation](/climbing-areas/canada-del-capricho/).
