Most crags in Tenerife are free to access, but a few protected areas need a permit, and some are closed to climbing. Always check before you go, because rules and nesting closures change.

## Crags that need a permit

| Crag | Permit | Limit | How to book |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Guaria](/climbing-areas/guaria/) (Guía de Isora) | Free, valid only for the chosen day | 70 climbers/day, up to 90 days ahead | [centralreservas.tenerife.es](https://centralreservas.tenerife.es/?a=4) |
| El Roquito (Roque de Jama) | Free, valid only for the chosen day | 10 climbers/day, up to 90 days ahead | [centralreservas.tenerife.es](https://centralreservas.tenerife.es/?a=4) |
| [Cañada del Capricho](/climbing-areas/canada-del-capricho/) (Teide National Park) | Individual reservation | 58 people/day | [Tenerife ON](https://www.tenerifeon.es/en/routes/climbing/canada-del-el-capricho-climbing-area) |

## Where climbing is not allowed

- **Los Gigantes cliffs**: protected natural area, climbing not permitted.
- **Martiánez** (Puerto de la Cruz): permanently closed.
- **New routes in Teide National Park**: no new bolting or route equipping is allowed.

## Nesting and wildlife closures

Authorities can close routes or sectors to protect nesting birds of prey. If you see a falcon nesting on a sector, don't climb there, even if it isn't signed. In Teide National Park some sectors close during the annual mouflon control.

## Climb responsibly

- Park considerately and never block access for farmers or residents.
- Take all rubbish home, including tape, fruit peel and cigarette ends.
- Use toilets before you go, and pack out paper.
- Keep noise down, and keep dogs under control.
- Don't add or remove bolts on existing routes. Re-bolting is coordinated locally, often funded by guidebook sales and donations.
