  # ── Getting started ─────────────────────────────────────────────
  - group: Getting started
    q: Can I go rock climbing in Tenerife with no experience?
    a: Yes. Our beginner climbing day is designed for people who have never climbed. A guide teaches you everything on the day, provides all the equipment and sets up safe top-ropes on easy routes.
  - group: Getting started
    q: Is rock climbing safe for beginners?
    a: Climbing with a qualified guide is very safe. Beginners climb on top-rope, so the rope is always anchored above you, and the guide checks every harness, knot and belay. Most accidents happen to unsupervised beginners who skip these checks.
  - group: Getting started
    q: How fit do I need to be to try climbing?
    a: Normal everyday fitness is enough. Climbing is more about technique and balance than strength. You need to be able to walk 10–20 minutes on uneven paths to reach the crag.
  - group: Getting started
    q: I'm afraid of heights. Is climbing for me?
    a: Many beginners are. You set the pace, nobody forces you to the top, and once you trust the rope most people relax quickly. Being nervous at first is completely normal.
  - group: Getting started
    q: What is the minimum age to go climbing?
    a: Children from about 6 years old usually enjoy our family session, which is always with a parent or guardian. Tell us the ages when you book and we'll confirm the best option.
  - group: Getting started
    q: Is there an upper age limit?
    a: No. Fit, active people of any age climb. Tell us about any health issues when you book and we'll adapt the day.
  - group: Getting started
    q: Can I climb if I'm overweight or not very sporty?
    a: Yes. Our harnesses fit a wide range of body sizes, and we choose slabby, less steep routes where your legs do most of the work. Tell us your height and weight when you book so we bring the right equipment.

  # ── Courses & progression ───────────────────────────────────────
  - group: Climbing courses
    q: What climbing courses can I take in Tenerife?
    a: We offer indoor-to-outdoor transition, learn to lead on bolts, and safety skills (anchors, lowering off and abseiling), plus multi-pitch instruction on long routes. See our climbing courses page for details.
  - group: Climbing courses
    q: I climb indoors. Do I need a course to climb outside?
    a: It's strongly recommended. Outdoor climbing adds skills you don't use in a gym, such as reading the rock, checking fixed gear, threading and lowering off, and dealing with loose rock. Our indoor-to-outdoor course covers all of this in a day.
  - group: Climbing courses
    q: How long does it take to learn to lead climb?
    a: Most climbers who already top-rope and belay confidently can lead safely on easy bolted routes after a 1–2 day course, then build experience. Leading at your top-rope limit takes longer.
  - group: Climbing courses
    q: Will I be able to climb on my own after a course?
    a: After our learn-to-lead and safety skills courses most people can climb independently on well-bolted single-pitch sport routes. We recommend starting well below your limit, and we give you a list of suitable crags.
  - group: Climbing courses
    q: Do I get a certificate?
    a: Our courses are practical skills training rather than a formal qualification. If you need a certificate for a club or gym, ask us before booking.
  - group: Climbing courses
    q: Can I learn trad or multi-pitch climbing in Tenerife?
    a: Yes. Las Vegas has trad crack lines for learning gear placements, and Guaria has long sport and trad routes for multi-pitch. We teach both as private courses.

  # ── Gear & what to bring ────────────────────────────────────────
  - group: Gear and clothing
    q: Is climbing equipment included?
    a: Yes. All our experiences include harness, helmet, ropes and safety gear. Beginner and family sessions also include climbing shoes.
  - group: Gear and clothing
    q: What should I wear for rock climbing?
    a: Comfortable sportswear that lets you lift your knees, trainers for the walk-in, and layers. A light jacket is useful for belaying in the shade or wind. Avoid long jewellery.
  - group: Gear and clothing
    q: What should I bring on a climbing day in Tenerife?
    a: At least 1.5–2 L of water per person, snacks or lunch, a sun hat, high-SPF sunscreen and sunglasses. The sun in Tenerife is stronger than in central or northern Europe, even in winter.
  - group: Gear and clothing
    q: Can I rent climbing gear in Tenerife to climb on my own?
    a: Yes. The Roxtar shop in Villa de Arico rents shoes, harnesses, quickdraws, belay devices, crash pads and clip sticks. Ropes are rented under special conditions. See our shops and gear rental guide.
  - group: Gear and clothing
    q: What rope length do I need in Tenerife?
    a: A 60 m rope covers most single-pitch sport routes. Some longer routes, especially in Arico Abajo and Guaria, need a 70 m rope, so check the topo and always tie a knot in the end of the rope.

  # ── Weather & season ───────────────────────────────────────────
  - group: Weather and season
    q: When is the best time to climb in Tenerife?
    a: October to May is the main climbing season in the south, with daytime temperatures around 18–24 °C. In summer it is hot on the coast, so climb early, follow the shade or head up to the high-altitude crags of Teide National Park.
  - group: Weather and season
    q: Can you climb in Tenerife in winter?
    a: Yes. Winter is peak season. The south gets sun on most days, rain is infrequent and usually short, and the rock dries fast. Only the high-altitude crags in Teide National Park can be cold or icy.
  - group: Weather and season
    q: Can you climb in Tenerife in summer?
    a: Yes, with planning. Start at sunrise, choose shaded walls and stop by early afternoon, or climb at altitude in Cañada del Capricho, which is much cooler than the coast.
  - group: Weather and season
    q: What if it rains or there's a calima?
    a: Rain in the south is rare and passes quickly. During calima (Saharan dust and heat) or strong wind we change crag, reschedule, or cancel with a full refund if conditions are unsafe.
  - group: Weather and season
    q: Is the north or the south of Tenerife better for climbing?
    a: The south and south-east (Arico, Fasnia, Granadilla) have most of the routes and the driest, sunniest weather. The north is greener and more often cloudy and humid, especially in winter.

  # ── Crags & rules ──────────────────────────────────────────────
  - group: Crags, permits and rules
    q: Where are the best places to climb in Tenerife?
    a: Arico is the main area, with 260+ sport routes. El Río, Tamadaya, Fasnia and other southern gorges add hundreds more. Guaria has long routes (permit required) and Cañada del Capricho is in Teide National Park (reservation required). See our climbing areas guide.
  - group: Crags, permits and rules
    q: How many climbing routes are there in Tenerife?
    a: Around 1,300 bolted sport routes appear in the current guidebook, and online databases such as TheTopo list over 2,000 routes including bouldering, trad and deep water soloing.
  - group: Crags, permits and rules
    q: Do I need a permit to climb in Tenerife?
    a: Most crags are free to access. Guaria and El Roquito de Jama need a free daily permit from the Cabildo's central reservations, and Cañada del Capricho in Teide National Park needs a reservation through Tenerife ON. When you climb with us we handle permits.
  - group: Crags, permits and rules
    q: Can you climb at Los Gigantes?
    a: No. The cliffs of Los Gigantes are in a protected natural area where climbing is not permitted. Martiánez in Puerto de la Cruz is also permanently closed.
  - group: Crags, permits and rules
    q: Where can I find topos for Tenerife climbing?
    a: The printed guidebook Escalada en Tenerife (sold at Roxtar in Arico), and the online databases theCrag and TheTopo. Our topos and guidebooks page links to all of them.

    a: We recommend travel insurance that covers outdoor activities such as rock climbing. Members of alpine clubs (e.g. Alpenverein, DAV, BMC) often have mountain rescue cover through their membership.
