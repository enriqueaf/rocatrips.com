Arico is the heart of Tenerife climbing. The volcanic gorges (*barrancos*) around the village of Villa de Arico hold the island's largest concentration of bolted routes, almost all of them single pitch with a fixed lower-off. Approaches are short, the rock is sound volcanic tuff and basalt, and the village has everything a climber needs: a climbing shop, climbers' hostels and cafés within walking distance.

## Sectors

The climbing is split into **Arico Arriba** (upper) and **Arico Abajo** (lower), each with many sub-sectors such as *Escuela* and *Peña del Lunes*. Routes in Arico Abajo are generally longer, and the hardest lines on the island are concentrated there. Arico Arriba has a high density of moderate routes, which makes it a great first stop.

For sector-by-sector topos use [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/spain/tenerife/arico) or the printed guidebook *Escalada en Tenerife* (see [topos and guidebooks](/plan-your-trip/topos-and-guidebooks/)).

## Conditions

Arico sits on the sunny south-east side of the island, sheltered from the cloud that often covers the north. From October to May temperatures are ideal for climbing. In summer the gorges get hot in the middle of the day: start early, follow the shade and drink more than you think. Rain is rare, and the rock dries quickly.

## Getting there and parking

Villa de Arico is about half an hour's drive from Tenerife South airport via the TF-1 motorway. A car is essential, because public transport doesn't reach the crags. Park only in obvious spaces, never block farm tracks or gates, and leave nothing visible in the car.
