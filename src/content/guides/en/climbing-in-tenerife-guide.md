## Why climb in Tenerife

Tenerife combines three things few European destinations offer together: **reliable winter sun**, **a huge amount of well-bolted rock** and **short approaches**. While crags in the Alps are under snow, the south of Tenerife has climbing temperatures around 18–24 °C and sun on most days. That's why climbers from Austria, Germany, the UK and Scandinavia have made it a winter classic.

The rock is volcanic: compact basalt and welded tuff in deep gorges (*barrancos*), with pockets, edges and slabs. Most routes are single-pitch sport climbs with fixed lower-offs, 15–30 m long, across every grade from 4 to 8b+.

## The climbing areas

- **[Arico](/climbing-areas/arico/)**: the centre of Tenerife climbing, with 260+ routes and short walk-ins.
- **[El Río](/climbing-areas/el-rio/)**, **[Fasnia](/climbing-areas/fasnia/)**, **[La Galería](/climbing-areas/la-galeria/)**, **[Fantasma](/climbing-areas/fantasma/)**, **[Zona Zero](/climbing-areas/zona-zero/)**: more southern gorges, each with 40–150 routes.
- **[Tamadaya](/climbing-areas/tamadaya/)**: the classic beginner and family crag.
- **[Las Vegas](/climbing-areas/las-vegas/)**: the best trad crag in the south.
- **[Guaria](/climbing-areas/guaria/)**: the big wall in the south-west, for experienced climbers only, with a permit required.
- **[Cañada del Capricho](/climbing-areas/canada-del-capricho/)**: high-altitude climbing in Teide National Park, with a reservation required.
- **[Bouldering](/climbing-areas/bouldering-tenerife/)**: 700+ problems at Arico Nuevo, Arico Ortiz and El Porís.
- **[Deep water soloing](/climbing-areas/deep-water-soloing/)**: a handful of summer lines.

See the full table on the [climbing areas](/climbing-areas/) page.

## When to go

October to May is ideal on the south-coast crags, and November to March is peak season. Summer is hot on the coast: climb at dawn, in the shade, or at altitude in Teide National Park. Full details: [best time to climb in Tenerife](/plan-your-trip/best-time-to-climb-tenerife/).

## Grades

French sport grades. Austrians and Germans can convert from UIAA with our [grade table](/plan-your-trip/grades-and-levels/). There's plenty in the 5–6b range, and a good selection of 7s and 8s, mostly in Arico Abajo and Guaria.

## Permits and rules

Most crags are free. **Guaria** and **El Roquito de Jama** need a free daily permit, and **Cañada del Capricho** needs a reservation. **Los Gigantes** and **Martiánez** are closed to climbing. Details: [climbing rules and access](/plan-your-trip/climbing-rules-and-access/).

## Guidebooks and topos

The local guidebook *Escalada en Tenerife* (sold at Roxtar in Arico), the Desnivel *Tenerife Sport Climbing*, and the online databases [theCrag](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/spain/tenerife) and [TheTopo](https://thetopo.com/areas/tenerife). See [topos and guidebooks](/plan-your-trip/topos-and-guidebooks/).

## Gear, shops and rental

Bring a 70 m rope and 12–14 quickdraws, or rent locally at Roxtar in Villa de Arico, which also rents crash pads. See [what to bring](/plan-your-trip/what-to-bring/) and [shops and gear rental](/plan-your-trip/climbing-shops-and-gear-rental/).

## Getting around and where to stay

Fly to Tenerife South (TFS) and hire a car, because public transport doesn't reach the crags. Stay in Villa de Arico (e.g. Tenerife Climbing House) for a pure climbing trip, or in the resorts for a mixed holiday. See [getting there](/plan-your-trip/getting-there-and-transfers/) and [where to stay](/plan-your-trip/where-to-stay/).

## Rest days

Hike in Teide National Park or the Anaga mountains, swim in the Atlantic, or visit La Laguna's old town and its [climbing gyms](/plan-your-trip/climbing-gyms/).

## Climbing with a guide

A guide makes the most sense if you're new to climbing, moving from indoor to outdoor, travelling without gear, climbing with kids, or want multi-pitch and permit areas without the hassle. We pick the crag for your level and that day's sun and wind. See [our experiences](/climbing-tenerife/).
